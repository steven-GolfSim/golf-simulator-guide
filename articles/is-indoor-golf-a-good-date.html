Is Indoor Golf a Good Date?

If you are looking for a date idea that is more interesting than dinner and less awkward than a movie, a golf simulator session might be exactly the right call. It works whether both of you play golf or neither of you does.

Published September 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Yes. Indoor golf is interactive, low-pressure, and naturally creates conversation without forcing it. Private simulator bays give you a relaxed setting where you can talk, laugh, and do something together without an audience.

Why it works as a date

Most first dates have a structure problem. Dinner puts you across a table with nothing to do but talk, which can feel like an interview. A movie gives you something to do but no chance to talk at all. A simulator session splits the difference. You stand side by side, take turns hitting, react to the shots on screen, and chat in between. The activity gives you something to talk about without the pressure of carrying a conversation for two straight hours.

You do not need to know how to play

This is probably the biggest misconception about a simulator date. You do not need any golf experience to have a good time. Simulators offer casual game modes, closest-to-the-pin challenges, and target games that are built for fun rather than scoring. Clubs are available to rent at most venues. The on-screen feedback shows where every shot goes, so there is instant entertainment even when (especially when) the shots go sideways.

The setting helps

Most simulator venues give you a private or semi-private bay. That means no crowd watching you swing, no noise from other tables, and no one rushing you to finish. Many venues also serve food and drinks, so you can order something mid-session without leaving to find another spot. It keeps the whole evening in one place, which makes it feel easy rather than scheduled.

How to keep it fun

The key is to treat it as a social activity, not a competition. Skip the full 18-hole round and play casual games or closest-to-the-pin contests instead. Make lighthearted bets on who gets closer to the target. Take turns coaching each other. If one person is more experienced, that is actually a plus because teaching someone a basic swing is a natural way to break the touch barrier and share a laugh. Build in a food or drink break halfway through and let the conversation breathe.

Planning tips

Book for about 60 to 90 minutes. That is long enough to settle in and have fun without the session dragging. Evenings and weekends fill up quickly at most venues, so reserve a few days ahead. Wear comfortable clothes you can move in. Normal trainers or sneakers are fine. Before you book, check whether the venue has club rentals and food service so you do not have to bring anything or plan a second stop.
