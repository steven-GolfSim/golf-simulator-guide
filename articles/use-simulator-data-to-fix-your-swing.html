Swing improvement

How to Use Your Simulator Data to Fix Your Swing

A simulator screen throws a lot of numbers at you after every shot. Club speed, ball speed, smash factor, launch angle, spin rate, attack angle, club path, face angle, carry distance, and more. Trying to work on all of them at once leads nowhere. Each metric points to a specific cause, and the trick is reading the right one for your miss, making one small change, and watching it respond.

Published November 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Pick the one number tied to your biggest miss. If you slice, read club path and face angle together. If you balloon the ball, check spin rate and dynamic loft. If distance is short despite good swing speed, look at smash factor. Work on that single metric until it improves, then move on.

Speed and contact

Club speed tells you how fast the head is moving, ball speed tells you how fast the ball leaves the face, and smash factor is the ratio between the two. Smash factor is your single best readout for how cleanly you struck the ball. A low number means energy is being lost to off-center hits, and adding more swing speed will not help until you improve contact first.

Launch and spin

Launch angle, spin rate, spin axis, dynamic loft, and spin loft work together to shape ball flight. If your shots balloon and fall short, spin rate is probably too high for the launch angle. If the ball drops out of the air quickly or will not hold a green, the combination needs adjusting. These numbers explain trajectory problems that look mysterious when you only watch the ball on screen.

Swing direction

Attack angle, club path, face angle, and face-to-path are the numbers that determine shot shape. The ball starts roughly where the face is pointing and curves away from the club path. Most shape problems come from face and path being too far apart. If you bring them closer together, the flight straightens out. Check face-to-path first. That single number tells you how much the ball is curving and in which direction.

Landing results

Carry distance, total distance, apex height, and landing angle round out the picture. Use your average carry distance for each club to build an honest yardage chart, not your best shot. Landing angle tells you which clubs will actually stop on a green and which ones will roll through. These are the numbers that translate directly to on-course decisions.

Diagnosing a slice or hook

Read club path and face angle together. If the path is well left of the face angle (for a right-handed player), the ball will slice. If the path is well right of the face, it will hook. The face-to-path number combines both into one reading. Bringing that number closer to zero straightens the flight without having to think about two metrics at once.

Focus on one number at a time

Work on the one metric tied to your biggest miss. Occasionally you will watch a second number alongside it, but chasing several at once leads to conflicting swing changes. Pick one, make a small adjustment, hit a few shots, and watch how that number responds. Pair it with a repeatable drill so the change carries from rep to rep. Once that metric improves, move to the next one.

When to get a coach involved

You can start on your own with the approach above, but an instructor who works with live data can identify which number to tackle first and how to change it without creating new problems. Indoor lessons with a launch monitor running give you immediate feedback on every adjustment.
