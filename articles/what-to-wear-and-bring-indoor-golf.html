What to Wear and Bring to Indoor Golf

Indoor golf is refreshingly low-maintenance. Wear comfortable clothes you can swing freely in, bring very little, and let the venue handle the rest. This guide covers the essentials so you can travel light and focus on hitting.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Wear athletic or smart-casual clothes you can make a full swing in, clean flat-soled shoes, and bring a glove if you use one. Clubs, balls, tees, and the screen are all provided by the venue.

What to wear to a golf simulator

Anything you can make a full, unrestricted swing in works. Athletic wear and normal golf attire are both fine, so there is no need to buy anything new for a first visit. Most bays are climate-controlled, so layers you can add or remove are a smart choice. Comfort and freedom of movement across your shoulders and hips matter far more than dress code.

What shoes to wear on a hitting mat

Footwear is the one detail worth a moment's thought. Clean, flat-soled athletic shoes grip the mat well and give you a stable base through the swing. Metal spikes are usually not allowed indoors because they can damage the mats and flooring. Soft spikes, spikeless golf shoes, or ordinary trainers with a flat sole all work. Make sure the soles are clean and dry so you do not slip mid-swing.

Do you need to bring your own clubs?

Not necessarily. Many venues offer club rentals, so you can show up empty-handed and still play a full round. If you already own a set, bringing it gives you the truest read on your game since the data will reflect the clubs you actually play. For a casual outing, rentals keep things simple. For serious practice or a fitting, your own clubs are the better call.

What the venue already provides

More than you might expect, which is why packing is so light. The bay supplies balls, tees, the hitting mat, the screen, and the simulator itself. Most places also have somewhere to set down your bag and jacket, and many offer food and drink. Because amenities vary from one venue to the next, check the specific listing or call ahead before you arrive.

What to actually pack

Beyond clubs, a short personal kit covers almost everything. Most of it fits in a jacket pocket or a small bag: a glove if you wear one, water or a drink, a small towel for your hands and grips, your phone for saving shot data, and a removable layer in case the bay runs warm or cool. That is genuinely all you need.
