Is Indoor Golf Good for Kids and Families?

Golf simulators have quietly become one of the best family-friendly activities you can book on a weeknight or a rainy weekend. The combination of instant visual feedback, casual game modes, and a safe indoor environment makes them work for kids in a way that a driving range or a full golf course rarely does.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Yes. Kids can start as soon as they can safely swing a club and enjoy the back-and-forth of a game. Simulators keep sessions short and game-based, which holds attention far better than a quiet afternoon on the range.

Why simulators work for kids

The biggest draw is the screen. Every shot shows the ball flying through a virtual course in real time, with distance, direction, and landing spot displayed instantly. That kind of immediate feedback keeps children engaged in a way that watching a real ball disappear into a field does not. Add in casual game modes, closest-to-the-pin challenges, and longest-drive competitions, and a simulator session starts to feel more like an arcade visit than a golf lesson. There is no pressure to play quietly, no slow groups ahead, and no long walks between holes.

Age and equipment

There is no strict minimum age. If a child can hold a club and swing it safely, they can play. Most venues rent junior-sized clubs, so there is no need to invest in equipment before you know whether your child even likes it. Younger kids do best with shorter sessions focused on fun rather than technique. Fifteen to twenty minutes of active swinging is plenty for a five or six year old. The goal is to leave while they are still having fun so they want to come back.

Keeping it fun for the whole family

Skip the idea of playing a full eighteen holes. Quick-format games and skills challenges give everyone more swings in less time and keep the energy up. Let each person take a turn, cheer the results, and rotate. If you have mixed ages in the group, team-based formats work well because a parent and a child can play together without anyone sitting out. Book one bay for the family, which keeps costs down since pricing is by the bay and the hour rather than per person.

Practical tips for parents

Weekday afternoons and off-peak times are usually quieter, which means less waiting and a more relaxed atmosphere for younger kids. If you are visiting on a weekend, book ahead. Check the venue listing on GolfSim.co to see whether they offer junior programs or family-specific packages. Safety-wise, simulators are one of the safer ways for a child to swing a club. The bay is enclosed, there is netting or a screen catching every shot, and you are standing right there. Standard supervision applies: one person swings at a time, and everyone else stays clear of the hitting area.
