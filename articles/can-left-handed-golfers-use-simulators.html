Accessibility & setup

Can Left-Handed Golfers Use Simulators?

Yes. Left-handed golfers can use essentially every modern golf simulator. The launch monitors behind the major systems all support left-handed play. The only real variables are how the physical bay is set up and whether switching between righty and lefty takes a moment of adjustment.

Published August 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: For overhead and behind-the-ball systems, switching to left-handed play usually takes no adjustment at all, just a settings toggle. A few floor-standing units sit beside the ball and need to be physically repositioned for a lefty. The bigger practical risk is club rentals, not the technology itself.

Do golf simulators work for left-handed golfers?

Thoroughly. The radar and camera technologies found in commercial venues are all designed for both handedness, and left-handed play is a settings toggle rather than a limitation. Overhead-mounted camera systems watch the hitting area from above and generally do not care which way you face. Radar units positioned behind the ball track flight the same in either direction. The handful of exceptions are practical, not fundamental: a floor unit that sits beside the ball needs to sit on the other side for a lefty, and a bay physically arranged around right-handed traffic may want a moment of rearranging.

Which launch monitor setups handle lefties best?

The smoothest are overhead camera systems and behind-the-ball radar. Both typically handle a lefty stepping in with a settings change or nothing at all, which also makes them ideal for mixed groups. Side-positioned photometric units are the ones that involve moving hardware, since the cameras must face the golfer's ball-side. Venues using them usually know the drill and reposition in under a minute.

What should lefties check before booking a bay?

Three quick questions. Does the bay support left-handed play without hassle, and if the venue runs different systems in different bays, which one should you book? Second, is there room: a minority of home-style setups are built tight against a side wall assuming right-handed swings, and a lefty needs the mirror-image clearance. Third, rentals, covered below. Most commercial venues will answer all three instantly, and the good ones note it on your booking.

Do lefties need to bring their own clubs?

Here is the practical catch: the simulator supports you, but the rental rack might not. House club sets skew right-handed everywhere in golf, and while many venues keep at least one left-handed set, few keep several, and fewer keep lefty options across flexes or junior sizes. If you depend on rentals, call ahead and reserve the lefty set explicitly. If you own clubs, bring them, your own equipment makes the data more meaningful anyway.

Does simulator data mean anything different for lefties?

The physics is identical; only the signs flip. Metrics like ball speed, carry distance, and spin read the same regardless of handedness. Directional metrics like face angle and club path are usually reported as open/closed or in-to-out relative to the target line, so the concepts transfer directly. Just remember that a slice curves left-to-right for a righty and right-to-left for you, so translate any right-handed instruction content accordingly. Most software handles the mirroring cleanly once the left-handed setting is on.
