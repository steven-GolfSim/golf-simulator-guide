Short game & accuracy

Do Simulators Measure Putting and the Short Game Accurately?

Simulators are excellent for full swings and very good for most short-game shots, but putting and the most delicate touch shots are the hardest things to reproduce indoors. Low-speed, low-spin shots give the sensor much less to measure than a full swing does.

Published August 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Wedge shots read well because they still produce measurable speed and spin. Putting is modeled rather than measured, since there is almost no data for a launch monitor to read from a slow putt. Use the simulator for wedge distances and contact, and use a real green for touch and reading break.

Why is putting hard to simulate?

A putt has very little ball speed and almost no spin, so there is far less data for a launch monitor to read, and the virtual green's break has to be modeled by the software rather than felt underfoot. Many venues handle this with a simplified putting interface or by letting you finish short putts automatically, which keeps play moving without pretending to perfectly read a tricky downhill putt. It is one area where the indoor experience is more of a convenience feature than a faithful practice tool, and that is fine once you expect it.

Are chipping and pitching accurate?

Partial wedge shots read better than putts because they still produce measurable speed and spin, so spin rate and carry distance on pitches and chips are genuinely useful for dialing in distances. The one thing the screen cannot give you is how the ball reacts on a real green after it lands, the release, the check, the way it feeds off a slope. Treat indoor wedge work as distance and contact practice, then trust the course for what happens once the ball is down.

What skills transfer best to the course?

The most transferable skill is repeatable distance for a given swing length. If you can hit a wedge a consistent carry indoors, that carry travels with you outside, because it is driven by your strike rather than the room. Solid contact transfers too. A clean strike is a clean strike anywhere. What does not transfer is green reading and the touch around the green, which depend on a real surface you can walk, feel, and study for yourself.

How do simulators handle green reading and roll-out?

Most systems model the green rather than measure it, drawing the break and speed from the course file instead of a real surface. That can be a good way to keep a round moving, but it is not the same as learning to read a putt you can crouch behind and feel. Use it to keep score and enjoy a round, and save your serious green reading for a real putting surface where the feedback is honest.

Can you groove wedge distances indoors?

This is where indoor short-game practice genuinely shines. Because a wedge still generates enough speed and spin to measure, you can rehearse a repeatable swing length for each carry and watch the numbers settle over a session. Pick a few target carries and groove the swing that produces each one. Those dialed-in distances are among the most valuable things you can take from a bay to the course, because scoring so often comes down to a precise wedge.

How should you practice the short game indoors?

Use the simulator for what it measures well, wedge distances and contact, and pair it with a real surface for touch. Many venues offer coaching that uses the data to tighten your wedge gaps, and some pair their bays with a practice green for putting. Build dependable wedge distances on the screen, then rehearse feel and green reading wherever you can find real turf.

Is indoor putting practice worth any time at all?

Yes, as long as you aim it at the right thing. A simulator will not teach you to read a subtle break, but it can still help you rehearse a smooth, repeatable stroke and steady tempo, and it keeps a virtual round flowing so you can practice the rest of your game. Think of it as stroke maintenance rather than green reading. For the touch and line-reading that win holes, a real surface remains the better teacher.
