Best Golf Grips for Sweaty Hands

Sweaty hands can make a worn golf grip feel slippery and encourage you to squeeze too hard. The right texture, size and care routine can provide better control without adding tension.

Published August 13, 2026 · Approximately 4 minutes

Quick answer: Corded and half-cord grips usually offer the most traction for sweaty hands. Textured all-weather rubber grips provide a softer compromise, while rain grips suit very wet conditions. Choose the correct size, keep a towel and suitable glove nearby, and replace grips once cleaning no longer restores traction.

What makes a grip work well with moisture?

Texture gives your hands or glove more surface to hold when moisture is present. Cord fibers create a rougher feel and strong traction, while patterned rubber channels and tacky compounds help all-weather grips remain usable in humid conditions.

There is a trade-off: aggressive texture can feel firm or abrasive, while a softer and smoother grip may feel comfortable but lose security sooner when damp. Test the feel on one club before regripping a full set.

Which grip type should you choose?

  • Full-cord grips: strong traction in sweat or rain, with the firmest and roughest feel.
  • Half-cord or hybrid grips: cord where the lead hand sits and softer rubber under the trail hand.
  • Textured all-weather rubber: a balanced choice for players who want moisture control without a heavily corded surface.
  • Rain or wet-weather grips: designed to maintain hold in very wet conditions, particularly when paired with rain gloves.
  • Smooth, soft grips: comfortable in dry weather but generally more vulnerable to sweat and wear.

Does grip size matter?

Yes. A grip that is too small can encourage you to clamp down, adding tension to the hands and forearms. A correctly fitted grip should feel secure with moderate pressure. Size depends on the grip model, hand dimensions and the tape layers installed beneath it, so a club fitter can help when standard sizing feels wrong.

How to stop grips becoming slick

  1. Carry a dry towel. Wipe your hands and grip before each shot on hot or humid days.
  2. Use the right glove. An all-weather or rain glove can improve security when a standard glove becomes damp.
  3. Rotate gloves. Switching between two gloves gives each one time to dry.
  4. Clean the grips. Mild soap and water can remove dirt, sunscreen and skin oils. Rinse and dry them fully.
  5. Store clubs properly. Avoid leaving them for long periods in a hot, damp vehicle or storage space.

When should grips be replaced?

Replace a grip when it looks shiny, feels hard, shows cracks or stays slippery after cleaning and drying. Wear depends on playing frequency, climate, storage and grip pressure, so a fixed calendar is less useful than regularly checking the surface and feel.

If one frequently used club wears faster than the rest, it is reasonable to replace that grip first rather than waiting for the entire set.

Can a simulator help you test a grip?

An indoor simulator provides a controlled place to compare feel across repeated swings. Look for twisting or slipping at impact and notice whether you can maintain control with lighter pressure. Ball-flight or face-angle inconsistency may reveal a problem, but comfort and fit still matter—data alone cannot choose a grip for you.

Frequently asked questions

What golf grips are best for sweaty hands?

Corded or half-cord grips usually deliver the strongest traction. Textured all-weather rubber is a good alternative when comfort is equally important.

Do gloves and towels still matter?

Yes. Even a moisture-resistant grip performs better when your hands, glove and grip surface are kept as dry and clean as possible.

Should you buy a full set immediately?

Not necessarily. Install the grip on one frequently used club and test its texture, size and comfort before changing every club.
