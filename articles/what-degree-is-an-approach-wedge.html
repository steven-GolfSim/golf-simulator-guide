What Degree Is an Approach Wedge?

An approach wedge normally fills the loft and distance gap between a pitching wedge and sand wedge. Its name can vary, so the number on the club matters more than the letter.

Published August 14, 2026 · Approximately 4 minutes

Quick answer: An approach wedge is usually 50–52 degrees. It is generally the same club as a gap wedge and may be marked A, AW, G, GW or simply with its loft. Because manufacturers use different specifications, check your actual club and measure its carry distance before deciding whether it fits your set.

What is the typical approach-wedge loft?

Most approach wedges sit between 50 and 52 degrees, but there is no universal standard. Some iron sets use stronger pitching-wedge lofts and may include an approach wedge below 50 degrees, while specialty wedge setups may use a slightly higher loft.

Use the manufacturer’s specifications or the loft printed on the club rather than assuming every A-wedge has the same number.

Is an approach wedge the same as a gap wedge?

For practical purposes, yes. The terms approach wedge and gap wedge normally describe the club between a pitching wedge and sand wedge. Manufacturers may stamp it with A or AW for approach, G or GW for gap, or display the loft directly.

The letter is mostly a naming choice. Loft, sole design and actual carry distance determine how the club performs in your set.

How does it compare with other wedges?

  • Pitching wedge: commonly around 44–48°, though modern sets can be stronger.
  • Approach or gap wedge: usually around 50–52°.
  • Sand wedge: commonly around 54–58°.
  • Lob wedge: commonly around 58–64°.

As loft increases, a comparable swing generally produces a higher, shorter shot. Actual results also depend on club construction, shaft length, ball, strike and swing delivery.

How should you choose the right loft?

  1. Find your pitching-wedge loft. Do not rely only on the club’s name.
  2. Check your sand-wedge loft. This defines the other end of the space you need to fill.
  3. Start with even loft gaps. Roughly four to six degrees between neighboring wedges is a common guide.
  4. Measure carry distances. Loft spacing is useful only if it produces playable yardage gaps.
  5. Consider the shots you use. Full swings, partial approaches and greenside shots may favor different sole or bounce designs.

Why carry distance matters more than the label

Two wedges separated by several degrees can still fly unexpectedly similar distances because of differences in club construction or strike. The opposite can also happen: a modest loft change may create a large yardage gap.

Record several solid shots with each wedge and compare average carry—not total distance or the single longest shot. The goal is a predictable progression that gives you a confident club choice for common approach yardages.

How can a simulator help?

A simulator or launch monitor can display carry distance, launch and spin for each wedge in consistent conditions. Hit a group of shots with the pitching, approach and sand wedges, remove obvious mishits, then compare the typical carry numbers.

If two clubs overlap or one gap is much larger than the others, a fitter may recommend bending a loft where appropriate or changing one wedge. Measurements are more reliable than assuming the stamped letters create even distances.

Frequently asked questions

What degree is an approach wedge?

Most are 50–52 degrees, but the exact loft varies. Check the club or manufacturer’s specification.

Is an A-wedge different from a gap wedge?

Usually not. A-wedge and gap wedge generally name the same position in the set, although specifications can differ.

How much loft should separate wedges?

Four to six degrees is a useful starting point. Confirm the setup by measuring actual carry distances with your normal swing.
