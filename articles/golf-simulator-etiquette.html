Golf Simulator Etiquette: The Unwritten Rules of Indoor Golf

A golf course spreads across 150 acres. A simulator bay fits inside a single room. That smaller space changes the stakes on everything from safety to noise to how you treat the equipment. Most of the etiquette is common sense, but a quick rundown before your first session helps everyone in the bay have a better time.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Stay out of the hitting zone when someone else is swinging, keep your turns moving so the group stays on schedule, treat the screen and sensors like expensive equipment (because they are), and let staff handle any technical problems.

Safety comes first in a small space

The single most important rule is that only one person should be in the hitting zone at a time. Nobody steps forward until the hitter has finished the swing and moved back. Drinks and phones stay off the mat, children and spectators sit well behind the person swinging, and everyone stays aware of where the club is at all times. On a golf course a mishit rolls into the rough. In a bay it can hit a wall, a screen, or a person standing two feet away.

Keep your turns moving

Most simulator sessions are booked by the hour, and other groups may be waiting for your bay. Choose your club while someone else is hitting rather than standing on the mat thinking about it. Use gimme or concede settings during longer rounds so short putts do not eat up time. Save the long conversations and phone breaks for between turns. None of this means rushing your swing, it just means being ready when it is your turn so the group stays on pace and gets the most out of the time they paid for.

Treat the equipment like it costs what it costs

Impact screens, launch monitors, and projectors are expensive and surprisingly easy to knock out of calibration. Always hit off the mat, never off the floor, and do not try to take real divots the way you would on turf. If the sensor starts misreading shots or the software freezes, tell the staff rather than adjusting the hardware yourself. One well-meaning attempt to fix a camera angle can throw off an entire calibration. If you are using rental clubs, treat them the same way you would treat your own set.

Be a good neighbor in shared spaces

Many simulator venues have multiple bays side by side with other groups nearby. Keep celebrations fun but not disruptive. Clean up your area when you are done. If the bays are close together, stay out of the neighboring group's line of sight while they are hitting. Small courtesies go a long way in a space where everyone is within earshot of everyone else.

Bringing guests and first-timers

If you are bringing someone who has never used a simulator, cover the safety basics before anyone starts swinging. Set the software to forgiving settings so they are not frustrated by a system that penalizes every topped shot. Let them take their time without hovering over their stance, and save the swing advice unless they ask for it. The goal of their first session is to enjoy it enough to want to come back.
