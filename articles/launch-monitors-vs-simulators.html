Launch Monitors vs Golf Simulators: What's the Difference?

The two terms get used interchangeably, but they describe very different setups. Understanding what each one actually does will save you from spending money on the wrong thing or walking into a venue expecting something it does not offer.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: A launch monitor is the sensor that measures your swing and ball data at impact. A golf simulator wraps that sensor inside a full experience: course software, a projection screen, and a hitting bay where you can play virtual rounds. Every simulator contains a launch monitor, but a launch monitor on its own is a practice and data tool, not a course you can play.

What a launch monitor does

A launch monitor is a sensor, either radar-based, camera-based, or a combination of both, that captures data the instant club meets ball. It measures metrics like ball speed, spin rate, launch angle, and carry distance. That data is what makes it invaluable for club fitting, deliberate practice, and tracking improvement over time. You can use one at the driving range, in your garage, or at a fitting studio. It gives you numbers. It does not give you a golf course.

What a simulator adds

A simulator takes the same sensor data and feeds it into course software that renders a full 3D environment on a screen or projector. You see the ball fly, land, roll, and stop on a virtual version of a real course. You get scorekeeping, shot tracking, and the ability to play eighteen holes with friends on a Saturday night without leaving the building. The launch monitor is the camera; the simulator is the whole cinema. That extra layer of software, display hardware, and enclosure is what separates a data tool from an entertainment and training experience.

When each one fits

If your goal is pure practice, a launch monitor may be all you need. It lets you hit the same shot over and over, compare clubs side by side, and watch your numbers change as you make swing adjustments. If you want to play courses, compete with friends, or test your course management under pressure, a full simulator is the better fit. Most indoor golf venues operate full simulators, which means you get both the data and the experience in one session. For a home setup, the choice comes down to space, budget, and whether you care more about practice data or playing rounds.

What to look for at a venue

When booking a bay at an indoor golf venue, the simulator brand determines what launch monitor is built in and how accurate the data will be. Venues running Trackman, Full Swing, or Uneekor systems use high-end sensors that deliver reliable numbers alongside the course experience.
