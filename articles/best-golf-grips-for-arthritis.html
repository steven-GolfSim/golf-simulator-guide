Best Golf Grips for Arthritis

The best golf grips for arthritis are oversized, soft and tacky. Here is what to look for so you can hold the club with less pressure and finish every round comfortably.

If your hands hurt by the back nine, the grip is usually the first thing worth changing. Arthritic hands need less squeezing, less vibration, and a surface that holds on even when your pressure is light. The right grip makes all three happen without changing anything else about your game.

Here is a quick look at what actually matters when choosing grips for arthritis.

Three Things That Make a Grip Arthritis-Friendly

Size. A larger diameter grip fills the hand more completely, so your fingers do not have to curl as tightly to hold on. Less curl means less joint strain on every swing. Midsize is the usual starting point. Jumbo is worth trying if your hands are large or the pain is significant.

Softness. Softer rubber compounds cushion the vibration that travels up the shaft at impact. Stiff, worn grips send that shock straight into sore joints. Fresh, soft rubber absorbs it. You will feel the difference immediately, especially on mishits.

Tack. A tacky or textured surface does some of the holding for you. When the grip is sticky, you can keep your hands relaxed without worrying about the club slipping. Worn-smooth grips force exactly the tight squeeze arthritic hands need to avoid.

Midsize or Jumbo?

Midsize is where most golfers with arthritis start. It adds enough diameter to reduce finger strain without dramatically changing how the club feels through impact. Jumbo grips go further and are worth testing if midsize does not provide enough relief. The trade-off is that very large grips can quiet the hands' natural release, which some golfers notice in their ball flight. That is usually a fair exchange for playing pain-free.

Two More Things That Help

Wear a glove on each hand. The extra cushion and traction reduce how hard you need to hold on. And if you are still playing with steel shafts, graphite transmits noticeably less vibration. Both changes are small on their own but stack well with the right grip.

Test Before You Commit

Regrip one club and play with it for a couple of weeks before ordering thirteen more. A simulator bay is ideal for this since you can hit the same shot repeatedly in comfort and feel how the grip behaves over a full session rather than a few swings in a shop.
