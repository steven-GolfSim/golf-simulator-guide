Best Private Golf Courses in Palm Springs

The Palm Springs area is home to some of the most striking private golf layouts in the country. Spread across the Coachella Valley from Palm Desert to La Quinta, these courses sit among desert canyons, boulder-lined ridges, and mountain backdrops that most public courses simply cannot offer.

Published November 20, 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Five private courses stand out in the wider Palm Springs area: Stone Eagle Golf Club in Palm Desert, Tradition Golf Club in La Quinta, BIGHORN Golf Club in Palm Desert, Toscana Country Club in Indian Wells, and The Madison Club in La Quinta. Each is a member-and-guest course, so access requires an invitation or membership.

Stone Eagle Golf Club

Located in Palm Desert, Stone Eagle is a Tom Doak design built into the natural hillside. Rather than reshaping the land, the course works around existing boulders, rock formations, and elevation changes. The result is a layout that feels raw and sculptural compared to more manicured desert courses. It is a walking-friendly design, which is unusual for the area.

Tradition Golf Club

Tradition sits in La Quinta and plays through desert washes, serpentine waste bunkers, and double fairways. Boulder landscaping and water features break up the holes, creating a course with more visual variety than many desert layouts. The design rewards route planning off the tee rather than pure distance.

BIGHORN Golf Club

BIGHORN offers two full 18-hole courses in Palm Desert. The Mountains course, designed by Arthur Hills, plays through elevated terrain with long canyon views. The Canyons course, designed by Tom Fazio, works through tighter corridors and more varied shot shapes. The two-course setup and practice facilities make it a strong option for members who want variety without leaving one property.

Toscana Country Club

Toscana in Indian Wells features two Jack Nicklaus-designed courses. The South course uses elevation changes to create strong risk-reward moments, while the North course plays through arroyos and native desert vegetation. The two layouts cover enough range that experienced players can find challenge in both, without either feeling repetitive.

The Madison Club

The Madison Club in La Quinta is a Tom Fazio design inside a private residential community. It is one of the more exclusive options on this list, built around a members-only clubhouse and positioned as a golf-centered second-home destination. The course itself is well-regarded for its conditioning and setting against the Santa Rosa Mountains.

What to know before visiting

All five of these are member-and-guest courses. That means you will need an invitation from a current member or a confirmed reciprocal arrangement. Before you go, it is worth confirming dress code, whether carts are required or optional, the guest fee structure, and any tee-time restrictions for non-members. Do not assume that staying at a nearby resort or hotel automatically grants access.

Frequently asked questions

Can I play these courses without a membership?

Only as a guest of a current member, unless a specific club runs an occasional guest event or reciprocal program. Contact the club directly to ask about access options.

Which course is best for a first visit to Palm Springs?

If you have the choice, BIGHORN offers two courses and strong practice facilities, which gives you the most golf in a single visit. Stone Eagle stands out for its design approach if you prefer something more natural and less resort-like.

Are there public courses nearby if I cannot get access?

Yes. The Coachella Valley has well-regarded public and resort courses as well. For those options, see the companion guide to public courses in Palm Springs.
