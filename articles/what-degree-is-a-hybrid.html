What Degree Is a 3, 4 or 5 Hybrid?

Hybrids are easier to hit than long irons, but their numbering is not standardized across brands. A 4 hybrid from one manufacturer may carry a different loft than a 4 hybrid from another. Understanding the actual degrees behind each number is what matters when you are building a set that flows smoothly from fairway woods down through your irons.

Published September 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: A 3 hybrid is typically 19 to 21 degrees, a 4 hybrid is 22 to 24 degrees, and a 5 hybrid is 25 to 27 degrees. Match hybrids to the irons they replace by loft, not by the number printed on the sole.

Typical hybrid lofts

Most manufacturers build hybrids within these ranges: a 2 hybrid sits around 16 to 18 degrees, a 3 hybrid around 19 to 21, a 4 hybrid around 22 to 24, and a 5 hybrid around 25 to 27. These numbers overlap with long iron lofts, which is the point. Hybrids are designed to replace the irons that most golfers struggle to hit consistently.

The key detail is that hybrid numbers do not line up neatly with iron numbers, and they differ between brands. A 3 hybrid from one company might carry the same loft as a 4 hybrid from another. Always check the actual degree stamp or spec sheet rather than trusting the number on the club.

Which iron does each hybrid replace?

As a general guide, a 3 hybrid replaces a 3 or 4 iron, a 4 hybrid replaces a 4 or 5 iron, and a 5 hybrid replaces a 5 or 6 iron. The overlap happens because iron lofts have gotten stronger over the years, so a modern 5 iron often carries the same loft that a 4 iron had a decade ago. Match by loft degree, not by the number, and you will avoid carrying two clubs that fly the same distance.

Why hybrids fly differently from irons

Even when a hybrid and an iron share the same loft, the hybrid will usually launch higher and carry slightly farther. That comes down to club design. Hybrids have deeper clubheads with a lower center of gravity, which gets the ball airborne more easily. They also have longer shafts than the irons they replace, which adds clubhead speed. For most golfers, that combination means a higher ball flight, a softer landing angle, and more forgiveness on off-center hits.

How to choose which hybrids to carry

Start with your longest iron that you hit consistently well. If your 5 iron is solid but your 4 iron is a struggle, replace the 4 iron with a hybrid of the same loft. Work upward from there. Some players replace everything above a 6 iron with hybrids. Others keep a 5 iron and carry one hybrid to bridge the gap to their fairway woods. There is no single right answer, just the setup that produces even distance spacing across your bag.

Confirm your gaps on a simulator

A launch monitor removes the guesswork. Hit 10 to 15 shots with each club and compare the average carry distances. You want consistent gaps of roughly 10 to 15 yards between clubs. If two clubs produce overlapping distances, one of them is redundant. If there is a gap of 25 yards or more between two clubs, you are missing a slot. Simulator sessions are ideal for this kind of testing because every shot is measured and you can compare side by side without losing balls.
