Simulator Technology

Trackman vs Full Swing vs Uneekor

These three brands show up in simulator venues across the country, and golfers often want to know which one is best before they book. The honest answer is that the differences matter less than most people think, but they do exist and they are worth understanding.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Trackman uses Doppler radar paired with a camera. Full Swing runs radar-based systems with a portable option combining radar and high-speed camera. Uneekor mounts overhead and uses high-speed cameras with optical sensors. All three deliver strong ball and club data, and real-world accuracy depends more on installation and calibration than on brand name.

How each brand measures your shot

The technology behind each system is genuinely different. Trackman pairs Doppler radar with an optical camera to track both the ball in flight and the club at impact. Full Swing uses radar-based simulators in its commercial installations and offers a portable unit called the KIT that combines radar with a high-speed camera. Uneekor takes a different approach entirely, mounting overhead and using high-speed cameras with optical sensors to capture the moment of impact from above. Each method has strengths, but for most players at a venue the differences in feel are subtle.

Club data is the real differentiator

Every one of these systems gives you solid ball data: speed, launch angle, spin, and trajectory. That part is table stakes. Where brands separate themselves is club data. Club speed, club path, face angle, and attack angle are the metrics that matter if you are working on your swing or getting fit for equipment. All three of these brands publicly document their club data capabilities, which puts them ahead of many other systems on the market. If club data matters to you, any of the three is a strong option.

Which one is the most accurate?

There is no universal winner here, and anyone who tells you one brand is categorically more accurate than the others is oversimplifying. Real-world accuracy depends heavily on how a unit is installed, calibrated, and maintained at a given venue. A perfectly set up Uneekor will outperform a poorly calibrated Trackman, and vice versa. The best way to judge accuracy is whether the numbers the system gives you feel consistent and match your real-world experience on the course. If the distances and shot shapes look right to you, the system is doing its job.

How much does each cost to play?

The price you pay for a simulator session is set by the venue, not by the launch monitor inside. A venue running Trackman in a high-rent area will charge more than a venue running the same system in a smaller market. Bay rates are driven by location, demand, time of day, and what the venue includes in the booking. Do not choose a venue based on brand alone and assume it will cost more or less because of the technology. Compare what you are getting for the hourly rate and pick the venue that gives you the best overall experience.

Which brand should you choose?

For casual rounds and social outings, the software and the venue matter more than the sensor. All three brands run simulation platforms with plenty of courses and game modes. For focused practice, lessons, or club fitting, lean toward whichever brand gives you the club data you want and feels trustworthy with its numbers. And in either case, pay attention to the venue itself. Bay size, mat quality, screen condition, food and drink options, and overall atmosphere will shape your experience more than the logo on the launch monitor.
