How to Choose a Golf Simulator Brand

With several well-known simulator brands on the market, the choice can feel overwhelming. The good news is that brand selection comes down to matching the technology to how you actually want to play, not chasing the most famous name in the room.

Published September 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Focus on the type of data the system captures and whether that matches your goals. For casual play and social outings, the brand barely matters. For serious practice and club fitting, the club data a system provides is what separates one brand from another.

The three main technology types

Most simulator brands use one of three sensor technologies: radar, camera-based (photometric), or overhead camera systems. Each has trade-offs and none is universally better than the others. Radar units track the ball through its full flight and tend to do well with ball speed and carry distance. Camera-based systems capture impact conditions with high precision and often deliver stronger club data. Overhead setups sit above the hitting area and measure from a different angle. The right choice depends on what you want to use the data for, not on which technology sounds more impressive.

Ball data vs club data

Every simulator system on the market gives you solid ball data: speed, launch angle, spin, and flight trajectory. That is the baseline and it is not really where brands differentiate themselves. The real separation comes from club data. Club speed, club path, face angle, and attack angle are the metrics that matter for improving your swing and getting properly fit for equipment. If those numbers matter to you, compare how each brand documents its club data capabilities. If you just want to play rounds and have fun, the ball data alone is more than enough.

Casual play vs serious practice

For social outings, group events, and casual rounds, the simulator brand matters much less than people assume. The software provides the games and the venue provides the atmosphere. Most platforms feel very similar when you are just playing a virtual round with friends. For dedicated practice, lessons, or club fitting work, the system's accuracy with club delivery metrics becomes important. That is where it pays to look deeper into what a brand actually measures versus what it estimates.

The venue matters as much as the brand

Even the best launch monitor in the world does not save a session in a cramped bay with a worn-out mat and poor lighting. When you are choosing where to play, do not fixate on which brand a venue runs. Pay equal attention to the bay size, mat quality, screen condition, projector brightness, and the overall comfort of the space. A well-maintained venue with a mid-tier system will usually give you a better experience than a run-down venue with a premium one.

How to decide

Start by being honest about which metrics you will actually use. If you are not going to study club path data after every session, there is no reason to choose a brand based on it. If you are serious about improvement, look for brands that publicly document what they measure and how. Try a few different systems at different venues before committing to a home purchase. And remember that the numbers should feel trustworthy to you. If a system consistently shows distances or spins that do not match your real-world experience, it is not the right fit regardless of its reputation.
