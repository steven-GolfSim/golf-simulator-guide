How Long Does a Simulator Round Take?

One of the most common questions before booking a simulator bay is how much time you actually need. The good news is that indoor golf moves faster than a round on the course, but the exact timing depends on a few things you can plan around.

Published September 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: A solo player can finish 18 holes in roughly 60 to 90 minutes on a simulator. A group of four will need closer to two hours or more depending on pace. Nine holes takes roughly half that time.

Why simulator rounds are faster

The biggest time saver is that the course comes to you. There is no walking or riding between shots, no waiting for the group ahead, and no time lost searching for a ball in the rough. You hit, watch the ball fly on screen, and hit again. The pace is entirely in your hands, which is why indoor rounds consistently run shorter than their outdoor equivalent.

How group size changes the math

Group size is the single biggest factor in how long a round takes. A solo player controls the pace completely and can move through holes quickly. A pair adds some wait time between shots but still flows well. Once you get to three or four players, the session stretches meaningfully because everyone hits on every hole. If you are booking for a foursome and want a full 18, plan for the longer end of the venue's booking windows or consider playing nine holes instead.

Simulator settings that save time

Most simulators have options that can trim your round without taking away from the experience. Conceding short putts is the easiest one. If a putt is inside three or four feet, letting the simulator give it to you saves a surprising amount of time over 18 holes. Some systems also let you shorten holes, skip practice swings in the calculation, or use a ready-to-go mode that speeds up transitions between shots.

Play vs practice sessions

A virtual round has a natural endpoint. You finish 9 or 18 holes and the session wraps up. Practice mode is open-ended, so you could spend 30 minutes on driver work or two hours cycling through your bag. If you are booking for practice rather than a round, the time you need is really about your goals for the session rather than a fixed number of holes.

How to plan your booking

The safest approach is to book conservatively. Most venues charge by time, so having a buffer at the end is better than rushing through the last few holes. If your venue allows it, ask whether you can finish the current hole if time runs out. Many places are flexible about that, and some simulators can even save your progress if you need to pick up where you left off later.
