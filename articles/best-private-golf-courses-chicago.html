Best Private Golf Courses in Chicago

Chicago's private golf scene runs deep. The area surrounding the city is home to clubs that have hosted U.S. Opens, Ryder Cups, and major amateur championships, alongside newer designs that use the region's rolling terrain and skyline views to full effect.

Published November 25, 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Four private courses stand out in the Chicago area: Chicago Highlands Club in Westchester, Medinah Country Club, Olympia Fields Country Club, and The Beverly Country Club. All four require membership or a member invitation to play.

Chicago Highlands Club

Located in Westchester, Chicago Highlands is an Arthur Hills design that makes the most of its elevation changes. The course offers views toward the Chicago skyline that are uncommon for the area, and several tee options give it range for different skill levels. The ninth hole in particular is often highlighted for how it uses the site's natural terrain. It is a relatively newer addition to the Chicago private golf landscape compared to the historic clubs on this list.

Medinah Country Club

Medinah sits west of Chicago and is one of the most recognized championship venues in the country. The club operates three full courses and extensive practice facilities, which gives members more variety than most single-course clubs can offer. Its tournament history runs across multiple decades and includes major professional and amateur events. Membership is invitation-only with member sponsorship, and attending a tournament at Medinah does not grant playing access.

Olympia Fields Country Club

Olympia Fields pairs two courses with distinct architectural personalities. The North course is a Willie Park Jr. design, while the South course comes from Tom Bendelow. That pairing makes the club an interesting stop for anyone who pays attention to course architecture, since the two layouts reflect different eras and design philosophies on the same property. The club's history includes a larger four-course inventory that has since been consolidated to the current two.

The Beverly Country Club

Beverly sits on the south side of Chicago and carries a long history of hosting major amateur and professional events. The club adopted a Donald Ross renovation plan that shaped much of the course's current character. It is a quieter name compared to Medinah or Olympia Fields, but it holds real weight among players who know Chicago golf history. There is no public daily-fee access.

What to know before visiting

All four courses are private. Access requires either a membership or an invitation from a current member. Before visiting, confirm the dress code, cart policy, guest fee structure, and any tee-time restrictions. Do not assume that reciprocal club arrangements, hotel stays, or living nearby will grant access to the course for a round.

Frequently asked questions

Can I play these courses without being a member?

Only as a guest of a current member. Some clubs may have reciprocal arrangements with other private clubs, but that varies and should be confirmed directly with the club.

Which course has the strongest championship history?

Medinah and Olympia Fields both have deep histories of hosting major professional events. Medinah's three-course setup and long tournament record put it at the top of that list.

Are there good public courses in Chicago if I cannot get private access?

Yes. The Chicago area has well-regarded public and municipal courses. For those options, see the companion guide to public courses in Chicago.
