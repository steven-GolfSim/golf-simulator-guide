Best Private Golf Courses in Raleigh

The Raleigh area's private golf scene stretches from one of the last Donald Ross designs ever built to multi-course clubs that give members genuine variety without leaving the Triangle. The five clubs on this list each bring something different, whether that is architectural history, sheer volume of holes, or a lakeside setting with a dedicated instruction program.

Published November 26, 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Five private courses stand out in the Raleigh area: Raleigh Country Club, Carolina Country Club, North Ridge Country Club, Prestonwood Country Club in Cary, and The Country Club at Wakefield Plantation. All five require membership or an approved guest arrangement to play.

Raleigh Country Club

Raleigh Country Club was established in 1948 and is recognized as the final Donald Ross design, making it one of the more architecturally significant private courses in North Carolina. The Ross pedigree gives the layout a character that reflects a specific era of course design. It is a separate club from Carolina Country Club, despite the shared naming history. Access requires membership or an approved guest arrangement.

Carolina Country Club

Carolina Country Club was incorporated in 1910 as Raleigh Country Club and was renamed in 1918. It is one of the oldest clubs in the area and pairs its long institutional history with a modern clubhouse. The club carries a deep local identity in Raleigh's golf scene. As with the others on this list, confirm your access directly with the club before planning a visit.

North Ridge Country Club

North Ridge offers 36 holes across nearly 400 acres, with the original course designed by George Cobb. The two-course setup gives members more variety for regular club play than most single-course facilities in the area can offer. The scale of the property also means less crowding during peak tee times. It is located in Raleigh.

Prestonwood Country Club

Prestonwood sits in Cary and operates 54 holes across three courses, each with different green-grass types. That three-course variety is unusual for any private club, and the family-oriented programming makes it a broader club experience beyond golf alone. While it is technically outside Raleigh proper, it falls squarely within the Triangle and serves many of the same members.

The Country Club at Wakefield Plantation

Wakefield Plantation is an 18-hole Hale Irwin design located beside Falls Lake in Raleigh. The course features pine-lined corridors and creek crossings that create a natural, wooded setting distinct from the more open layouts at some of the other clubs on this list. The club also runs a dedicated instruction program for members looking to work on their game alongside regular play.

What to know before visiting

All five courses are private. Access requires either a membership or an invitation from a current member. Before visiting, confirm the dress code, cart policy, guest fee structure, and any tee-time restrictions. Do not assume that reciprocal club arrangements or living in the Triangle will grant access for a round.

Frequently asked questions

Can I play these courses without being a member?

Only as an approved guest of a current member. Some clubs may have reciprocal arrangements with other private clubs, but that varies and should be confirmed directly.

Which course has the strongest architectural pedigree?

Raleigh Country Club, as it is recognized as the final Donald Ross design.

Are there good public courses in Raleigh if I cannot get private access?

Yes. The Raleigh area has well-regarded public and municipal courses. For those options, see the companion guide to public courses in Raleigh.
