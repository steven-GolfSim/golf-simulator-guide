Group events

Indoor Golf for Groups, Parties and Corporate Events

Simulator bays have become one of the easiest ways to get a group together for something more interesting than dinner and drinks. The format works for birthdays, bachelor parties, team outings, and client entertainment, and it does not require anyone in the group to actually be good at golf.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Indoor golf simulators are a natural fit for groups because the game modes are built for mixed skill levels, the bays are private and climate-controlled, and pricing is by the bay rather than per person. Book enough bays for your headcount, pick a casual format, and let the venue handle the rest.

Why simulators work for groups

The biggest advantage of a simulator bay for a group outing is that everyone can participate regardless of experience. Game modes like closest-to-the-pin, longest drive, and mini-games turn the session into a friendly competition where a first-timer can compete alongside a single-digit handicap. Players take turns hitting, which leaves natural downtime for conversation, food, and drinks. There is no walking, no waiting on slower groups ahead, and no weather to worry about. The bay is private, the temperature is comfortable, and the whole event stays in one place.

What kinds of events work

Birthday parties, bachelor and bachelorette outings, holiday gatherings, team-building events, and client entertainment all fit the format well. For corporate groups, simulators offer something more engaging than a conference room and more accessible than a full day on the course. A two-hour session gives everyone a chance to swing, socialize, and enjoy the experience without a major time commitment. Many venues also offer food and beverage packages, private event spaces, and dedicated staff for larger bookings.

Planning your session

Start with your headcount. Most bays comfortably hold four to six people at a time, so a group of twelve would want two or three bays running side by side. Share your numbers with the venue when you call so they can recommend the right setup. Book early, especially for weekends and evenings. Ask about rental clubs if not everyone in the group owns a set, and check whether the venue can handle food and drinks or whether you should plan that separately. A quick look at the venue listing on GolfSim.co will show you what each location offers.

Making it run smoothly

Keep the format casual. Quick-play games and skills challenges work better than trying to play a full eighteen holes, especially with larger groups where everyone is waiting for a turn. Assign someone to keep the rotation moving so no one sits idle for too long. If you have a mix of golfers and non-golfers, team-based formats keep everyone involved without putting anyone on the spot. The goal is for people to have fun, not to grind through a scorecard.
