Best Public Golf Courses in Phoenix

Phoenix is home to a range of public golf courses that go well beyond the standard desert layout. The courses on this list include a pine-tree-lined track that feels nothing like the surrounding Sonoran landscape, a city-owned course that doubles as a collegiate home venue, and a links-style design with mountain views and lake features near South Mountain.

Published November 26, 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Four public courses stand out in Phoenix: Raven Golf Club Phoenix, Papago Golf Club, GCU Golf Course, and Aguila Golf Course. All four are open to nonmember play, though booking windows, resident-card eligibility, and rates vary by course.

Raven Golf Club Phoenix

Raven Golf Club is a Gary Panks and David Graham design that stands apart from most Phoenix courses because of its pine tree setting. The mature stands of pines lining the fairways create a look and feel that is unusual for the Sonoran Desert. The course offers public visitor booking alongside membership options. Confirm current rates, practice facility inclusions, and any seasonal maintenance schedules before booking.

Papago Golf Club

Papago is a city-owned Billy Bell design set against a desert-butte backdrop. It serves as the home course for Arizona State's golf teams, which gives it a competitive pedigree that most municipal courses lack. The practice amenities are substantial for a public facility. Despite the collegiate connection, the course is fully open to public play. Check whether Phoenix resident-card eligibility affects your booking window or rate.

GCU Golf Course

Formerly known as Maryvale Golf Course, GCU Golf Course is open to the public, students, and local residents with no private-club requirement. The course has been updated under its current affiliation and provides a straightforward public option in the Phoenix area. Check for any course or range closures, and confirm which rate category applies to your visit.

Aguila Golf Course

Aguila is a Gary Panks links-style design located near South Mountain. The course features mountain views and three lakes that come into play on several holes, adding genuine water-hazard decisions to what is otherwise an open layout. When booking, make sure you are reserving the regulation 18-hole course and not the separate nine-hole par-three course. Confirm city-card eligibility and tee-time rules with the facility.

What to know before booking

All four courses are open to public play, but "public" means a nonmember booking option, not free golf or guaranteed availability. Booking windows, cancellation terms, cart or walking policies, and rental availability vary by course. Confirm total charges directly with each operator before your round, and check whether a Phoenix resident card affects your rate or tee-time access.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be a Phoenix resident to play these courses?

No. All four courses accept nonresident bookings, though residents with a city card may get earlier booking windows or lower rates at city-owned courses like Papago and Aguila.

Which course is the most unique?

Raven Golf Club Phoenix, for its pine-tree-lined fairways. It looks and plays very differently from the typical desert course you would expect in the area.

Are there indoor golf options in Phoenix?

Yes. Phoenix has several indoor golf simulator venues. For those options, see the companion guide to golf simulators in Phoenix.
