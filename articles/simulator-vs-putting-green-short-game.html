Simulator or Putting Green: Where Should You Practice Short Game?

The short game is where most strokes are saved, and it is also where the split between indoor and outdoor practice matters most. A simulator and a real putting green each cover different parts of the puzzle, and using both is the fastest way to improve around the greens.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Use the simulator for wedge distance control, spin consistency, and partial-swing gapping. Use a real putting green for reading break, developing touch, and practicing the feel shots that a screen cannot replicate. The best results come from splitting your time between both.

What the simulator does best

A simulator excels at measuring the things that make wedge play consistent: carry distance, spin rate, launch angle, and club speed. Even on partial swings, the sensor captures enough data to give you reliable numbers. That makes it the ideal place to build your yardage gaps, confirm that your 50-yard pitch actually goes 50 yards, and groove the feel of repeatable half and three-quarter swings. You can hit the same shot from the same distance over and over without walking to collect balls, which means more reps in less time.

What a real putting green does best

A real green gives you everything a screen cannot: slope you can feel through your feet, grain you can read with your eyes, and speed that changes with the weather and time of day. Putting on a simulator is useful for working on stroke mechanics and face control, but it will not teach you how to judge a four-footer with left-to-right break on a fast green. Touch, pace, and green reading are feel skills that only develop on real turf. The same goes for delicate chip shots where bounce, roll-out, and the firmness of the landing area determine the result.

How to split a practice session

The most productive approach is to start on the simulator while you are fresh and your focus is sharpest. Work through your wedge distances, vary your targets, and pay attention to the carry numbers. Once you have put in your reps on distance control, move to the putting green for feel work. Practice short putts from four to eight feet, hit some lag putts from distance, and play a few chip-and-putt games that force you to read the green and adjust to real conditions. This order works because the simulator builds the mechanical consistency, and the green layers in the feel and judgment that turn good contact into good scores.

Finding a venue with both

Some indoor golf venues offer both simulator bays and a practice putting green on site, which makes splitting a session easy. Not all venues have both, so it is worth checking the listing before you book. You can browse venues and their facilities on GolfSim.co to find a location that fits how you want to practice.
