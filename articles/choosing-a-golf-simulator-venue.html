How to Choose a Golf Simulator Venue

There are nearly 4,000 indoor golf simulator venues across the US. Most golfers pick one based on the brand name on the screen or whichever place is closest. Neither is a great strategy. The venue that fits how you actually play matters more than the logo or the drive.

Check the launch monitor system, bay size, pricing structure, rental availability, and recent reviews before booking. Technology sets the floor. Technology matters, but not as much as you think

The launch monitor sets the accuracy floor. Well-known systems like TrackMan, Full Swing, and Uneekor all produce a convincing experience, but they measure differently and some players develop a preference. Check which system a venue runs before booking if accuracy matters to you. For first-timers, the differences are subtle enough that any reputable system will do.

Bay size matters more than most people realize

You feel cramped within the first few swings. A good bay has enough ceiling height and width to swing a driver freely, a comfortable distance to the screen, and a hitting mat that does not punish your joints. If photos on the website look tight, they probably are. Check recent reviews for comments about space.

Match the venue to your actual plan

This is where most people go wrong. A venue that is perfect for a group night out is not always the right place for serious practice, and the reverse is true.

Social round: Look for comfortable seating, food and drink, and a relaxed atmosphere.

Focused practice: Look for range modes, detailed shot data, and quiet off-peak hours.

Lessons or club fitting: Look for venues that specifically advertise instruction or fitting services.

Groups and events: Look for places that handle private bookings and have enough bays for your party.

Being honest about what you actually want from the session prevents the most common disappointment.

Check the boring details early

The logistics are where a promising plan falls apart. Before you book, check:

  • Hourly rate and whether it changes at peak times
  • Booking method (online, phone, or walk-in)
  • Whether rental clubs are available
  • Group size limits per bay
  • Parking and accessibility

These vary by location even within the same brand, so read the specific venue listing rather than assuming.

Read reviews for patterns, not stars

A single five-star review tells you nothing. A pattern across 15 recent reviews tells you everything. Read for the things you care about: staff friendliness, equipment condition, accurate time slots, bay cleanliness. Pay attention to how recent the comments are, since a venue can change hands or upgrade and old reviews stop being relevant.

The simple rule

Pick the venue that fits how you will actually play, not the one with the most famous logo on the screen. Technology sets the floor. The room, the staff, and the booking experience decide the rest.
