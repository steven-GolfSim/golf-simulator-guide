What Is an Up and Down in Golf?

An up and down is when you miss the green, get the ball onto the putting surface with one shot, and hole the putt with the next. Two strokes from off the green into the cup. It is the single most important scoring skill that separates low-handicap golfers from everyone else.

Published August 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: "Up" means getting the ball onto the green with a chip, pitch, or bunker shot. "Down" means sinking the following putt. Do both in two total strokes and you have saved a stroke that most amateur golfers would lose. The stat is tracked professionally as "scrambling percentage."

What counts as an up and down?

Your ball is off the green. You play one shot that lands on the putting surface. You then hole the putt. That is an up and down. The first shot does the "up" work. The putt does the "down" work. The two strokes combined complete the save.

It does not matter how the first shot gets there. A bump and run, a high lob, a bunker blast, even a long pitch from 40 yards out. If it finishes on the green and you one-putt, it counts.

Why it matters more than most golfers think

Even good golfers miss greens. A scratch player misses roughly a third of greens in regulation. A 15-handicap misses more than half. What separates a bogey from a par on those holes is almost always the ability to get up and down.

PGA Tour players convert about 60 percent of their up-and-down attempts. The average amateur converts around 15 to 20 percent. That gap is the single biggest reason scores differ between skilled and recreational golfers. Closing even part of that gap drops your handicap faster than adding distance off the tee.

You get two chances, not one

Most golfers put all the pressure on the chip. They think they need to land it next to the pin to have any chance. That is backwards. An up and down gives you two separate opportunities to execute. You can hit a decent chip to eight feet and make the putt. Or you can hit a great chip to two feet and tap it in. Either path works.

Thinking of it as two chances instead of one removes the pressure to be perfect with the chip and frees you up to make a smart, controlled shot instead of a desperate one.

Three things that improve your conversion rate

Pick a landing spot, not a finishing spot. On chips and pitches, focus on where the ball first touches the green rather than where you want it to end up. Controlling the landing point is far more repeatable than trying to predict how much the ball will roll.

Use your most comfortable club. You do not need a lob wedge for every short shot. A 9-iron bump and run from just off the green is easier to execute than a high-lofted flop shot and achieves the same result with less risk.

Read the putt before you chip. Knowing where you want to leave the ball on the green is impossible if you have not looked at the putt first. Walk to the hole, read the slope, then chip with that information in your head.

How a simulator helps you practise this

A simulator is one of the best environments for building the core skill behind up-and-down play: distance control with wedges. You can hit the same 30-yard pitch over and over, watching the exact carry distance, spin rate, and landing angle on every shot. That data feedback loop is much faster than chipping to a practice green where you are guessing at distances.

You will not replicate the putting half of an up and down on a simulator, but the wedge control you build indoors translates directly to closer chips on the course, which means shorter putts and higher conversion rates.

Frequently asked questions

Is an up and down the same as a scramble?

They overlap but are not identical. "Scrambling" is the broader stat that covers making par or better after missing the green. An up and down specifically means doing it in exactly two strokes from off the green. A scramble could also include holing out directly from off the green in one shot.

Does an up and down have to save par?

No. The term describes the two-stroke sequence itself, not the score outcome. You can get up and down for bogey, double, or even birdie. But in common usage, golfers almost always mean saving par when they talk about getting up and down.

What is a good up-and-down percentage?

PGA Tour average is around 60 percent. For amateurs, anything above 30 percent is solid. Getting from 15 percent to 30 percent will likely drop your handicap by several strokes without changing anything else about your game.
