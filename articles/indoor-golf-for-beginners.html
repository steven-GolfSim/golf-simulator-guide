Indoor Golf for Beginners: Your First Visit

Never hit a ball at a simulator before? A golf simulator is one of the easiest ways to try golf for the first time. Indoor comfort, no weather to worry about, and an experience you can dial up or down to match how confident you feel.

Published September 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: You do not need your own clubs, a handicap, or any prior experience. Most venues rent full sets, staff walk you through the basics, and the private bay means no one is watching you mishit. Treat the first visit as a relaxed experiment rather than a test.

Do you need your own clubs or special gear?

No. Most venues offer rental sets, so there is no equipment barrier to trying a session. A glove is optional, not required. Renting first is also the easiest way to find out whether you actually enjoy it before spending money on your own clubs.

What should you bring?

  • Comfortable clothing you can swing freely in.
  • Clean athletic shoes. Golf spikes are not necessary indoors.
  • Your own clubs if you have them, though rentals work just fine.
  • A glove, if you prefer one, though it is optional.
  • Friends, if you want to split the cost and make it more fun.

What happens on your first visit?

Staff will walk you to a private bay with a hitting mat and screen. From there you can pick a difficulty level, warm up on a virtual range, or jump straight into a round. The bay is enclosed, so a mishit stays between you and the screen, not the whole venue.

How do you make the first session go well?

  • Warm up in range mode before playing a full round.
  • Choose a forgiving course and generous scoring settings.
  • Ask staff questions freely. They expect it from first-timers.
  • Bring people you enjoy spending time with.
  • Prioritize having fun over how well you play.
  • Ignore the on-screen numbers if they do not interest you yet.

What if you have never swung a club at all?

Indoor simulators are actually easier for absolute beginners than an outdoor course. There is no waiting on other groups, mishits have no real consequence, and optional coaching is available if you want faster progress. Many venues welcome complete beginners specifically because the environment is so forgiving.

Should you book solo or with friends?

Either works well. A group session brings a social atmosphere and splits the cost, while a solo visit gives you a quieter, more focused space to figure things out at your own pace. Pick whichever fits how you want to spend the time.

Do you need to understand the numbers on screen?

No. You can have a great first visit without glancing at a single number. The on-screen data is there if you get curious about it later, but it is entirely optional for enjoying the session.

The takeaway

Treat your first visit as a relaxed experiment, not a test. The goal is simply to make contact, laugh at the misses, and leave wanting to come back.
