The State of Indoor Golf in America: 2026

Indoor golf has gone from a niche novelty to a fast-growing segment of the golf industry, but how big is it really? GolfSim.co published an annual research report that puts numbers behind the growth, tracking venue density, new openings, technology adoption, and the competitive landscape across every US state and major metro.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: The report draws from a continuously tracked directory of US simulator venues, with the latest snapshot dated October 2026. It covers venue density per capita by state and metro, directory growth over time, franchise versus independent venue mix, average bays per venue, launch monitor technology adoption, and the demographic context of the cities where simulators cluster.

How many simulator venues exist and where they cluster

The report measures venue density per 100,000 residents at the state and metro level, which gives a more useful picture than raw venue counts. A state with 200 venues and a large population may actually be underserved compared to a smaller state with half as many locations. Metro-level data highlights the cities where indoor golf has the deepest footprint and where the market still has room to grow. Understanding which areas are saturated and which are underserved matters for anyone thinking about opening a new venue or investing in the space.

Directory growth and new venue openings

GolfSim.co has been tracking new venue listings since June 2026, and the report breaks down new additions by metro area. This growth data shows where the industry is expanding fastest and whether the pace of new openings is accelerating or leveling off. For operators and investors, this trend line is one of the most useful parts of the report because it signals which markets are attracting the most new competition.

Franchise versus independent venues

The indoor golf market includes both franchise operations and independently owned venues. The report tracks the mix between the two, which matters because it reflects how the industry is maturing. A market dominated by franchises looks very different from one built on small independent operators, and the balance between them affects pricing, technology choices, and the range of experiences available to golfers in a given area.

Simulator technology and launch monitor trends

Not all simulator venues run the same technology. The report examines which launch monitor brands and simulator platforms are most commonly deployed, how many bays the average venue operates, and whether venues are trending toward camera-based or radar-based systems. For golfers, this context helps when choosing a venue based on the type of data and experience you are looking for. For operators, it benchmarks where the industry is investing.

Where simulators meet demand

The report layers in demographic data for the cities where simulator venues are most concentrated. Population density, climate, median income, and existing golf infrastructure all play a role in determining where indoor golf thrives. Markets with harsh winters and strong golf cultures tend to have higher venue density, but the data also reveals some surprises in sunbelt cities where indoor golf is growing despite year-round outdoor access.
