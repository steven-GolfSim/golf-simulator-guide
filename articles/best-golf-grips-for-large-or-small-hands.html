Best Golf Grips for Large or Small Hands

Grip diameter is one of the quietest performance variables in golf. A difference of a few fractions of an inch changes how your hands engage the club, how much tension you carry through the swing, and ultimately where the ball goes.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Start with your glove size. Small or medium gloves point toward undersize or standard grips, large gloves toward standard or midsize, and XL or larger toward midsize or jumbo. When you wrap your fingers around the grip, the two middle fingers should rest lightly against the pad of your thumb without pressing hard into it.

Four standard sizes

Most grips fall into four diameter categories: undersize, standard, midsize, and jumbo. The actual dimensional differences between them are small, usually just a sixteenth of an inch from one category to the next, but the effect on feel and hand action is noticeable from the first swing. Undersize grips suit smaller hands and encourage active wrist release. Standard fits the widest range of golfers. Midsize reduces hand tension for players with larger palms, and jumbo grips quiet the hands almost entirely, which can help golfers who struggle with hooking.

What happens when the fit is wrong

A grip that is too thin for your hands allows the fingers to wrap too far around it, which tends to produce excessive hand action through impact. The result is often hooks or inconsistent face control. A grip that is too thick restricts the natural release of the club, leaving shots drifting weakly to the right for a right-handed player. Neither problem is dramatic enough to diagnose on feel alone, which is why so many golfers play the wrong size for years without realizing it.

Fine-tuning with build-up tape

You do not have to commit to a full grip swap to experiment. Each extra wrap of tape under the grip thickens the diameter slightly, letting you move between standard sizes in small increments. This is how most club fitters dial in a precise fit. It is inexpensive, reversible, and easy to do at home or at a pro shop.

Confirm it on a launch monitor

The most reliable way to know whether a grip change is helping is to measure what it does to your ball flight. An indoor golf simulator session lets you hit the same club with different grip setups under controlled conditions. The launch monitor will show changes in face angle and club path that your eyes alone would miss. Ten to twelve swings per setup gives you enough data to see a clear pattern, and the whole test takes less than half an hour.
