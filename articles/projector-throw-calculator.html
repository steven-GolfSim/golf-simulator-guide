Golf Simulator Projector Throw Calculator

Getting the projector placement right is one of the trickiest parts of a home golf simulator build. Mount it too close and the image spills off the screen. Mount it too far back and it sits in the middle of your swing path. GolfSim.co built a free calculator that takes the guesswork out of the equation.

What throw ratio means and why it matters

Throw ratio is the relationship between the projector's distance from the screen and the width of the image it produces. A projector with a 0.5:1 throw ratio placed five feet from the screen produces an image ten feet wide. A projector with a 1.0:1 ratio at the same distance produces an image only five feet wide. In a golf simulator room, space is usually tight, which means you need a projector with a short enough throw ratio to fill your screen from the distance you actually have available. The calculator handles this math for you by matching your screen size to the throw ratio of real projector models.

Why the throw needs to clear your swing

In most home simulator setups the projector mounts on the ceiling behind the golfer, projecting forward onto the impact screen. That means the projector beam passes over or beside your head and club during the swing. If the projector is too far back, you end up standing in the beam path and casting a shadow on the screen every time you swing. If it is mounted overhead but too low, the club can clip the housing on the backswing. The calculator factors in swing clearance so you know before you drill a single ceiling bracket whether the geometry actually works in your room.

Projector models the calculator supports

The calculator includes specs for several popular simulator projectors, with throw ratios verified as of July 2026. The range covers ultra-short-throw models like the ViewSonic LS831WU at 0.25:1, mid-range short-throw options from Optoma and BenQ in the 0.5 to 0.9 range, and standard short-throw models up to about 1.08:1. Resolutions span 1080p, WUXGA, and 4K UHD. Selecting your model from the list means the calculator uses the manufacturer's actual throw ratio rather than a generic estimate, which makes the result more accurate.

What to do with the result

Once the calculator returns your mounting distance, measure that distance in your room from the screen surface straight back along the ceiling. If the number fits your available space and the calculator confirms swing clearance, you have a workable setup. If the distance does not fit, you have two options: choose a projector with a shorter throw ratio, or reduce your screen size. Either adjustment brings the mounting point closer to the screen. Running a few combinations through the calculator before purchasing anything can save you from buying a projector that physically cannot work in your room.
