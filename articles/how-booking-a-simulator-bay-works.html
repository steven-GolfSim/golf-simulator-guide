How Does Booking a Simulator Bay Work?

Booking indoor golf is as simple as reserving a bowling lane. Pick a venue, choose a time and length, and the bay is yours. Here is how the process works so your first visit is smooth.

Published August 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Most venues take bookings online, by phone, or as walk-ins when a bay is free. You rent the bay by time (not per person), so a group shares one bay and splits the cost. Book ahead for evenings and weekends. Walk-ins work on quiet weekday afternoons but are never guaranteed at peak times.

Three ways to book

Online. The most common and most convenient. You see available slots, pick one, and confirm. Many venues link their booking system right on their listing page, so you can go from finding a place to securing a time in a few clicks.

By phone. Handy if you have questions about rentals, group size, or special requests. Some smaller venues still prefer phone bookings.

Walk-in. Works on quiet weekday mornings and afternoons. Do not rely on it for Friday night or weekends. If your plans hinge on a specific time, book ahead.

What you are actually paying for

You rent the bay for a block of time, not a seat per person. One bay, one price, regardless of whether one person or four people use it. That is why indoor golf is surprisingly affordable for groups: four players splitting a $60/hour bay are paying $15 each.

Pricing varies by venue and time of day. Peak hours (evenings, weekends) cost more than off-peak (weekday daytime). Some venues offer package rates for longer sessions that work out cheaper per hour than booking hourly.

How far ahead to book

Quiet times (weekday mornings, early afternoons): Same-day or next-day booking usually works. Sometimes walk-in is fine.

Peak times (evenings, weekends, winter season): Book as early as you can. Popular venues fill prime slots days in advance. If your plan involves coordinating a group, book the moment everyone confirms.

Rule of thumb: The busier the slot you want, the earlier you should book it.

What you need to book

Have these ready before you start so the process takes two minutes:

  • Preferred date and time (plus a backup)
  • How long you want the bay
  • How many people are coming
  • Whether anyone needs rental clubs
  • Payment details (some venues take a deposit for peak slots)

What happens when you arrive

Give your name at the counter. Staff will walk you to your bay and run through the basics: how the screen works, where the balls and tees are, how to start a round or switch to practice mode. If you reserved rental clubs, they will be waiting. The whole setup takes a few minutes, so arriving slightly early means you start hitting from minute one of your paid time instead of losing setup time.

Can you change or cancel?

Usually yes, though every venue handles it differently. Some allow free cancellation up to a certain point. Others hold deposits for prime slots and group bookings. Check the cancellation terms when you book, especially for weekend evenings or events. A quick message to the venue clears up any uncertainty.

Booking for a group

One person reserves a single bay and everyone shares it. Before booking, confirm the bay holds your group size comfortably. Most bays fit 2 to 6 players. For larger gatherings, ask whether the venue can set aside multiple bays or handles private events.

Book further ahead for groups since coordinating everyone's schedule gets harder last minute. Confirm rental clubs for anyone who needs them at the same time you reserve the bay.
