How Does Trackman Work?

Trackman works by using radar to track your club and the golf ball at impact, then converting those measurements into a realistic ball flight on screen. You never touch any of it. You make a normal swing and the system does the work in a fraction of a second.

Published August 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Trackman sends out a continuous radar signal and reads how the moving club and ball change that signal. From those changes it calculates club speed, ball speed, launch angle, and spin. Software then runs those numbers through a ball-flight model and lands the shot on a virtual hole.

What is Trackman?

Trackman is one of the best-known names in launch monitors and golf simulators. A launch monitor is the sensor that measures your club and ball, while a simulator wraps that sensor in software, a screen, and a hitting bay so you can play virtual courses. Trackman built its reputation on radar tracking outdoors, following the full flight of the ball down the range, and the same core technology now powers indoor simulator bays.

How does Trackman measure your shot?

Trackman uses Doppler radar. It continuously emits a radar signal and measures how that signal shifts as your club swings through impact and the ball launches away. From those shifts it works out the movement of both the club and the ball, measuring the strike rather than estimating it from a single snapshot. That is the same principle used to track full ball flight outdoors, scaled down to fit an indoor bay without losing the thread of the shot.

How does it work indoors in a bay?

Indoors there is no room for the ball to fly its full distance, so the radar reads the launch just after impact and the software models the rest of the trajectory. To stay precise in that short space, many indoor Trackman setups pair the radar with a camera, combining what the radar measures in motion with what a camera sees at the exact moment of impact.

What does Trackman measure?

The exact list depends on the model, but the readings fall into three groups:

  • Club delivery — club speed and club path through impact.
  • Ball launch — ball speed, launch angle, and spin rate, which together decide how the shot flies.
  • Results — the modelled carry distance and where the ball finishes on the virtual hole.

How does it turn measurements into a shot on screen?

Once the radar, and the camera where one is fitted, has measured your strike, Trackman's software runs those numbers through a ball-flight model and lands the shot on the virtual hole. The model applies the physics of how a real golf ball flies, so a well-struck shot climbs, curves, and rolls out the way it would outdoors.

Is Trackman accurate?

Radar launch monitors like Trackman are widely used for club fitting, coaching, and tour-level practice, so for everyday play and improvement the numbers are reliable enough to act on with confidence. How accurate a given bay feels still depends on calibration and the quality of the setup around it, from the mat to the screen distance. A great sensor in a poorly tuned room can still disappoint, and a carefully set-up bay can feel superb.

How is Trackman different from camera-based systems?

The main divide between simulator systems is how they see the shot. Radar units like Trackman track objects in motion, while camera-based units photograph the ball at the moment of impact. Radar likes a little distance to watch the ball move, while cameras capture everything right at contact and fit comfortably into tighter rooms. Neither is universally better. The right choice often comes down to the space and how well the unit is installed.

Do you need your own clubs?

No. You play a standard golf ball with normal clubs, so there is nothing special to bring. Many venues provide balls, and some offer loaner clubs too. Using your own set does have one upside: the data then reflects the exact clubs you actually play, which matters if you are mapping your distances or fitting gear.
