How Do Golf Simulators Work?

A golf simulator takes a real shot, measures what happened in the first moments after impact, and turns those measurements into a virtual ball flight on a screen. The swing is yours; the technology simply captures the strike and shows where the ball would have gone.

Published August 18, 2026 · Approximately 6 minutes

Quick answer: A golf simulator combines a launch monitor, hitting area, impact screen or net, display, and simulation software. The launch monitor records ball—and sometimes club—data at impact. The software then uses those measurements to calculate trajectory, carry, curve and roll before displaying the shot on a virtual range or course.

What happens after you hit the ball?

The process feels almost instant. You place a real golf ball on the mat, make a normal swing, and hit toward a screen or net. As the ball leaves the club face, the launch monitor captures the information it needs to describe the shot.

That data is sent to the simulator software, which calculates a flight path and renders the result on screen. Within a fraction of a second, you can see whether the shot started left or right, flew high or low, curved, carried the expected distance, and rolled after landing.

  1. You hit a real shot.
  2. The launch monitor measures it.
  3. The software calculates the flight.
  4. The display shows the result on a range or course.

The main parts of a golf simulator

A complete setup is more than a projector and a screen. Several pieces work together, and the overall experience is only as good as the way those pieces are installed and tuned.

  • Launch monitor: the sensor system that measures the shot.
  • Hitting mat: a stable surface that lets you swing indoors.
  • Impact screen or net: safely stops the golf ball after impact.
  • Projector or display: shows the virtual range, course and shot result.
  • Computer, tablet or console: runs the software and connects the hardware.
  • Simulator software: turns measured shot data into ball flight, course play, practice modes and scoring.

How does the launch monitor measure a shot?

Launch monitors typically use high-speed cameras, radar, or a combination of sensing technologies. Camera-based systems observe the ball and sometimes the club around impact. Radar-based systems track movement as the ball travels away from the hitting area.

The exact hardware differs by brand, but the goal is consistent: capture enough information to describe the ball's initial motion accurately.

What data does a golf simulator use?

  • Ball speed: how fast the ball leaves the club face.
  • Launch angle: how high or low the ball starts vertically.
  • Launch direction: whether the shot begins left, right or near the target line.
  • Spin: how the ball is rotating, which helps determine climb, curve and stopping behavior.
  • Club data: on systems that measure it, club speed, path and face information can add more detail.

How does the software create virtual ball flight?

Once the shot data arrives, simulator software applies a ball-flight model to estimate what would happen in open air. It calculates the trajectory from launch conditions, then accounts for forces such as gravity, lift and drag. The result is drawn in the virtual environment and continues through landing and rollout.

Why do some simulators feel more accurate than others?

Accuracy is not determined by one specification. The launch monitor matters most, but installation and environment also shape the result.

  • launch-monitor quality and calibration
  • the amount of room available for the sensor and player
  • ball-to-screen distance and bay dimensions
  • hitting-mat quality and stability
  • lighting and projector brightness
  • software settings and course physics

What can you do on a golf simulator?

  • play full virtual rounds
  • practice with immediate shot data
  • work through distance-control and target challenges
  • play closest-to-the-pin and group games
  • take lessons using measured feedback
  • compare clubs or complete a club-fitting session

Does simulator golf feel like real golf?

The swing can feel very close because you use normal clubs, a real ball and a full motion. What changes is the environment: you do not get changing wind, uneven lies, deep rough under your feet or the visual depth of an outdoor hole.

What should a first-timer look for?

Focus on practical details: enough room to swing comfortably, reliable equipment, a clean hitting mat, straightforward software, rental clubs if needed, and staff who can get you started quickly.

Frequently asked questions

How does a golf simulator know where the ball went?

The launch monitor measures the ball immediately after impact using cameras, radar or a combination of sensors. Software then converts those measurements into a calculated virtual flight.

What does a golf simulator measure?

Common measurements include ball speed, launch angle, direction and spin. More advanced systems may also measure club speed, path and face information.

Are golf simulators accurate?

A quality launch monitor in a properly configured bay can provide highly useful shot and distance feedback. Accuracy still depends on the sensor, calibration, room conditions, hitting surface and software.

Do you hit a real golf ball in a simulator?

Yes. In a standard simulator bay you hit a real golf ball with normal clubs into an impact screen or net while the launch monitor records the shot.
