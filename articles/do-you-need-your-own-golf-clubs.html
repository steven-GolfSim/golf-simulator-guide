Getting started

Do You Need Your Own Clubs for Indoor Golf?

It is one of the first questions people ask before booking a simulator bay, and the answer depends entirely on what you are there to do. Casual rounds and social sessions work fine with rental clubs. Serious practice and data tracking call for your own set.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: No. Most simulator venues offer rental clubs, so you can walk in without bringing anything. But if you are practicing with the intent to improve or tracking your numbers over time, your own clubs will give you far more accurate and useful data.

Renting clubs at the venue

Most indoor golf venues keep a selection of rental clubs available for walk-ins and first-time visitors. These are typically standard-length, regular-flex clubs that cover the basics: a driver, a few irons, and a putter. They are perfectly fine for a casual round with friends, a birthday party, or a first visit where you just want to see what simulator golf feels like. Some venues include rentals in the bay price, while others charge a small additional fee.

When to bring your own clubs

If your goal is practice rather than entertainment, bringing your own clubs is worth the effort. Shaft flex, grip size, club length, and lie angle all affect how the ball launches, and a simulator records every one of those variables at impact. When you use rental clubs, the data reflects those clubs, not your game. That means the carry distances, spin numbers, and shot shapes you see on screen will not match what you produce on a real course with your own set. For anyone working on a swing change, dialing in gapping, or preparing for a round, your own clubs are the only way to get numbers you can trust.

How your own clubs affect the data

A simulator measures what happens at impact and uses that data to project the ball flight. Club head speed, face angle, attack angle, and spin rate all feed the model. When the club in your hands matches the one you play on the course, the projection is as close to reality as the system can get. Switch to a rental club with a different shaft weight or flex, and those numbers shift. The simulator is still accurate, but it is accurately measuring a club that is not yours. That distinction matters if you are using the session to track improvement or make equipment decisions.

Beginners and special cases

If you are brand new to golf, rentals are the right call. There is no reason to invest in a set before you know whether you enjoy the game. Simulator sessions with rental clubs are a low-cost way to find out. Left-handed players and juniors should check the venue listing before booking, because not every venue stocks left-handed or junior-sized rentals. A quick look at the venue page on GolfSim.co will tell you what is available. Club fittings are a separate situation entirely: the fitter provides their own test clubs with interchangeable shafts and heads, so you leave your bag at home for those.
