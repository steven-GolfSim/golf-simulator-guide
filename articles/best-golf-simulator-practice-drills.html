The Best Practice Drills for a Simulator Session

A simulator session without a plan is just expensive ball-hitting. The drills that work best indoors all share one thing: they use the screen's instant feedback to give every shot a clear target and a number to chase. Here are five that translate directly to lower scores on the course.

Published September 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: The five best simulator drills are the smash-factor ladder for contact, the gapping test for distance control, the draw-and-fade game for shot shape, the wedge distance ladder for scoring shots, and pressure games to practice performing under stress.

Smash-factor ladder for solid contact

Pick one club and hit a series of shots while watching your smash factor, which is ball speed divided by club speed. The goal is not to swing harder but to keep that ratio consistent shot after shot. If the number drops, slow down until you find the tempo where you strike the center of the face reliably. Once you can hold a steady smash factor for 10 shots, move to the next club. This drill trains centeredness of strike, which improves both distance and accuracy at the same time.

Gapping test for distance control

Hit 10 shots with each club from your wedges through your driver and record the average carry distance for each. What you are looking for is even spacing between clubs, ideally 10 to 15 yards apart with no big gaps or overlaps. This builds a trustworthy yardage chart you can rely on during real rounds. If two clubs produce the same carry, one of them is redundant. If there is a 25-yard gap between two clubs, you are missing a slot.

Draw-and-fade game for shot shape

Pick one target and try to curve the ball in both directions. Call your intended shape before every swing, then check the spin axis number on screen to verify whether the ball actually moved the way you planned. This is one of the drills where simulators really shine, because you get immediate confirmation of whether you produced a draw or a fade rather than guessing from ball flight at the range.

Wedge distance ladder for scoring shots

Using a single wedge, hit controlled shots at three different carry targets, for example 60, 75, and 90 yards. Focus on landing the ball within a few yards of each number before moving to the next. Partial wedge shots are where a lot of strokes hide on the scorecard, and a simulator lets you measure the exact carry on every attempt. Pay attention to landing angle as well, since steeper approaches hold greens better.

Pressure games for scoring under stress

Assign yourself points based on hitting specific carry distances and start lines, then keep a running score across a set of 10 or 15 shots. Many simulator platforms also have built-in skills challenges or closest-to-the-pin modes that add natural competition. The goal is to blur the line between practice and real play so that pressure swings feel routine instead of different.

Getting the most from each session

Warm up for a few minutes, then pick one or two drills that target the weakest part of your game. Spend most of your booked time there rather than bouncing between everything. Finish with a few virtual holes to test what you worked on. Keep brief notes on your numbers so you can track progress across sessions. Change drills when they become easy, not before.
