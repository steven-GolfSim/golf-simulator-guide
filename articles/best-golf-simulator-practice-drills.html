The Best Practice Drills for a Simulator Session

The best simulator drills all share one trait: they use the screen's instant feedback to give every shot a clear target and a number to chase. That turns aimless ball-beating into focused practice you can measure from rep to rep.

Published October 2026 · Approximately 3 minutes

Quick answer: Five data-backed drills — a smash-factor ladder for contact, a gapping test for distance control, a draw-and-fade game for shot shape, a wedge distance ladder for the short game, and a scored challenge for pressure. Pick one or two per session, watch one number, and rotate.

What makes a good simulator drill?

A good drill has a clear target, a single number to watch, and a way to keep score so you can compare today with last time. Vague intentions do not stick, but a defined task — keep this number steady across a set of shots — gives your brain something concrete to solve. Repeatability matters too: a drill you can run the same way every visit lets you actually track progress.

Drill 1: Smash-factor ladder for solid contact

Hit one club and aim to keep smash factor consistent across a set of shots, slowing down if it drops. The number reflects how centered your strike is, giving immediate feedback on contact quality rather than guessing from sound or feel. Start with a comfortable club, find a swing speed that produces clean numbers, and only add effort once the strike stays consistent. When the number holds steady, step up a club and start the ladder again.

Drill 2: Gapping test for distance control

Hit several shots with each club from wedge to driver and record the average carry distance, then look at the spacing between clubs. The goal is reliable, evenly spaced gaps so you are never stuck between clubs on the course. Pay attention to averages rather than your single best strike, since the average is what you will reproduce under pressure. This one drill quietly fixes more scoring problems than most swing changes.

Drill 3: Draw-and-fade game for shot shaping

Pick a target and try to curve the ball both ways on command, using spin axis to confirm each shot actually bent the way you intended. Call your shape out loud before each swing, then check the screen. Feel can lie but the number does not. Over time you build the ability to move the ball on demand, which is one of the most satisfying skills a golfer can own.

Drill 4: Wedge distance ladder for the short game

Pick a wedge and try to land controlled shots at a series of different targets, watching carry distance and landing angle. Partial wedge shots are where a lot of strokes hide, and the simulator lets you rehearse them with feedback you simply cannot get on a range. Focus on hitting your numbers rather than your hardest swing, and treat consistency as the win.

Drill 5: Scored challenge for playing under pressure

Give yourself a score and some consequences. Skills challenges and closest-to-the-pin games turn a drill into something to beat, which forces you to commit the way you must on the course. Set a personal best and try to top it, or play a one-ball-per-target game where every shot counts. Pressure changes your swing, so practising with a little of it is how you make changes hold up when it matters.

Putting drills into a session

Warm up first, then pick one or two drills tied to your weakest area and spend the bulk of your time there before finishing with a few holes. Cramming every drill into one visit spreads your attention too thin, so rotate them across sessions instead. End on a virtual round to test whatever you drilled under real decisions. Keep a quick note of which drill you ran and the number you chased so the next session builds on this one.
